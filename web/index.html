Finding 1The group beats budget only because Portugal raised prices.

Year-to-date EBITDA is €4.41M against a budget of €4.17M (+5.7%). Portugal's 4% list-price rise adds €0.87M. Without it the group would be €0.63M, or 15%, below budget.

Finding 2Spain's hiring savings hide a larger miss.

Late sales hires keep salaries €0.14M under budget. The same gap cost Hydraulics volume and service revenue, and Spain's EBITDA is 12.6% short.

Finding 3The UK sells more in pounds and less in euros.

UK product sales are 1.6% above budget at the budget rate but 2.0% below in euros. Sterling averaged about 1.15 against a budget of 1.19, which cost €0.36M of sales.

Finding 4Two cost leaks are worth chasing.

A steel surcharge on bearings from May costs €0.32M of margin. Portugal's new outbound carrier, since March, costs another €0.17M. The checks also catch a CloudSuite invoice posted twice in the UK in June.

How it is built

  • The data are synthetic: a Python generator writes 81,000 journal lines, budgets, a reforecast, a sales sub-ledger and FX rates for a fictional company. Eight business stories are planted on purpose and listed in the README, so the findings can be checked.
  • dbt models on DuckDB turn the ledger into a star schema (entity, account hierarchy, cost centre, product line, month) and marts for the P&L, the bridge and the exceptions.
  • Tests check that every journal balances, that the sales sub-ledger agrees with the ledger to the cent, and that both bridges add up exactly to the variance they explain.
  • The same marts feed a Power BI model (about 90 DAX measures, a calculation group for month, quarter and year to date, a report-page tooltip, row-level security by entity) and a Qlik Sense app (set analysis, section access).
